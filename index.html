Complex coacervation,
from mechanism to application

The Srivastava Lab studies complex coacervation and its use in the design of soft materials. We study how macromolecular architecture, charge distribution, and solution conditions govern the length scales, structure, and dynamics of polyelectrolyte complexation, and we apply that understanding to coacervate protocells and membraneless compartments, bio-inspired adhesives and polyelectrolyte complex hydrogels for wet environments, and selective separation processes.

Research Areas

Coacervate protocells and membraneless compartments

Membraneless coacervate compartments with controlled size and salt stability, developed as models of prebiotic cells and as colloidal bioreactors.

Read more →

Bio-inspired adhesives and coacervate hydrogel scaffolds

Self-assembled coacervate networks that adhere and bear load in aqueous, saline, and physiological environments.

Read more →

Fundamentals of complex coacervation

Structure, phase behavior, and relaxation dynamics of polyelectrolyte complexes, and the scaling frameworks that describe them.

Read more →

Intelligent water treatment and selective separation technologies

Polyelectrolyte- and coacervate-based separations designed for selectivity between species rather than bulk removal.

Read more → Collaborative

Interfaces, thin films, and dense emulsions

Foam film stratification and intermicellar ordering, and the imaging and modeling of dense emulsions, with the Sharma and Peterson groups.

Read more → Collaborative

Sustainable composites and cements

Colloidal and interfacial design applied to upcycled polymers, low-carbon binders, and printable construction materials, with the Sant group and the UCLA Institute for Carbon Management.

Read more →

Application Areas

What we learn about electrostatic assembly becomes materials for medicine, water, and the built environment.
